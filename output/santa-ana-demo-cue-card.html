Speaker Script

Most community work does not start in a clean project management tool. It starts in scattered tabs, local notes, screenshots, field context, and conversations between people who each know part of the picture. For this demo, the shared place is a Santa Ana Watershed coop. The goal is not to replace local conservation groups. The goal is to help members prepare better coordination with them. First, the watershed organizer creates the coop. That gives the work a boundary: a purpose, a memory space, and a place where reviewed knowledge can eventually be shared. Then a restoration contributor joins by member invite. That matters because Coop is not just a solo AI notebook. Different members can bring different local context into the same shared coordination space. The organizer brings planning, governance, and support-path tabs. The contributor brings watershed, habitat, and restoration evidence. Each person rounds up their own browser context from their own profile. Now Coop turns that messy context into a private action brief. The important part is the review boundary: raw tabs, exact site details, volunteer logistics, and uncertain claims do not automatically become public. The brief gives the group a practical structure: who to coordinate with, what evidence to preserve, what support paths exist, and what learning could safely be shared back to the coop. Once the organizer reviews it, they publish only the safe version: public context, partner categories, next steps, and reusable learning. Then we switch to the contributor browser and show the shared memory update. That is the loop: two members, separate context, one coop, reviewed synthesis, and shared action memory the group controls.

Do Not Say

  • Do not say Coop auto-publishes community knowledge.
  • Do not say Coop replaces conservation districts.
  • Do not make the story only about grants.
  • Do not claim perfect privacy forever.
